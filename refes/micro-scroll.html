Structure & Complexity

A virus is a submicroscopic infectious agent that replicates only inside the living cells of an organism. Viruses infect all life forms, from animals and plants to microorganisms.

First identified in the late 19th century, viruses were initially defined as non-filterable infectious agents. Their structural complexity rivals that of the most intricate natural forms.

Form follows
function.

Every spike, every protein layer, every geometric facet exists for a precise biological purpose. The architecture of a virus is an evolutionary masterwork of lethal efficiency.

The coronavirus surface is studded with glycoprotein spikes that bind to ACE2 receptors on host cells. These crown-like protrusions, for which the family is named, are the primary mechanism of entry and infection.

The Rhinovirus is the most common viral infectious agent in humans and the predominant cause of the common cold. Spread worldwide, it thrives at temperatures slightly below core body temperature — which is why nasal passages, cooler by a few degrees, are its preferred habitat.

Symptoms include sore throat, runny nose, nasal congestion, sneezing and coughing — sometimes accompanied by muscle aches, fatigue, malaise, or headache. Fever and extreme exhaustion are more characteristic of influenza.
